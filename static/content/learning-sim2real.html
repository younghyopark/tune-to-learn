Sim-to-Real Transfer Degrades on Stiff, Overdamped Gains

Finally, we examine whether certain gain settings transfer more reliably from simulation to real hardware. We study reaching tasks with a Franka Research 3 robot to directly isolate the motor-level sim-to-real gap.

Stiff, overdamped gains are easiest to model…

To ensure a fair comparison across gain settings, we first perform gain-specific system identification. For each gain configuration $\mathbf{K}$, we excite the real robot with sinusoidal position targets $\mathbf{q}^{\text{des}}(t) = A\sin(\omega t)$ and optimize simulation parameters $\psi$ to match the resulting state trajectories:

$$\psi^\star(\mathbf{K}) = \arg\min_\psi \sum_{t=0}^{T} \|\mathbf{x}(t;\,\mathbf{K}) - \bar{\mathbf{x}}(t;\,\psi)\|^2$$

where $\mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{q},\,\dot{\mathbf{q}})$ denotes the real robot state and $\bar{\mathbf{x}}(\cdot;\psi)$ its simulated counterpart. The MSE after system identification is over an order of magnitude lower for stiff, overdamped gains compared to other settings. We attribute this to stiff, overdamped dynamics filtering out nonlinearities that are difficult to capture with idealized simulated actuators: high damping suppresses high-frequency effects such as joint flexibility and transmission dynamics, while high stiffness reduces sensitivity to steady-state errors from imperfect gravity compensation or stiction.

…but hardest to transfer.

For each gain setting, we train RL policies in the corresponding calibrated simulation and deploy them directly on the real robot without fine-tuning. We adapt the hyperparameter optimization from the RL section to account for real-world constraints:

$$h^\star(\mathbf{K}) = \arg\max_h \; \tilde{J}\!\bigl(\pi^\star(h;\,\mathbf{K})\bigr),$$

where $\tilde{J}$ augments the original objective with a penalty for violating real-world robot limits. We measure sim-to-real fidelity via trajectory error—the MSE between real and simulated state trajectories:

$$\mathcal{E} = \underbrace{\|\mathbf{q}_{\text{sim}} - \mathbf{q}_{\text{real}}\|^2}_{\text{position error}} + \underbrace{\|\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\text{sim}} - \dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\text{real}}\|^2}_{\text{velocity error}}$$

Paradoxically, stiff and overdamped gains—which yield the best system identification—produce the worst sim-to-real transfer. The dominant failure mode is high-frequency oscillation from the closed-loop interaction between the neural network policy and the aggressive controller, which amplifies small prediction errors into out-of-distribution states. Even domain randomization is insufficient to prevent this failure mode. In contrast, compliant and critically damped settings act as a natural low-pass filter, absorbing sim-to-real discrepancies and enabling significantly more reliable transfer.
